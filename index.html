Beyond Diameter

A deep generative model that learns the full 3D shape of the thoracic aorta, and finds the shape changes that a single diameter measurement misses.

  • Accepted for oral presentation at BMES 2026
  • Journal manuscript under review

Yufan (Viola) Wu, Yusuf A. Ozturk, Krashn Dwivedi, Alan Braverman, Fanwei Kong, Jessica Wagenseil
Washington University in St. Louis

The problem with one number

In Marfan syndrome (MFS) and Loeys-Dietz syndrome (LDS), aortic aneurysms are watched mainly through one measurement: the widest diameter of the aorta.

But these diseases do more than widen the aorta. They also change how long it is, how the arch curves, and how the descending aorta bends.

A single number can't see those changes. Two aortas with the same diameter can have very different shapes.

So we asked a simple question. If a model learns the whole shape, what can it tell us that diameter can't?

How it works

Each aorta is represented as a 3D point cloud: a set of points on its surface, taken from a CT scan. We trained on 518 point clouds from patients with MFS and LDS.

The model has three parts.

  • An encoder that summarizes the shape.A PointNet encoder reads the point cloud and compresses it into a short code of 16 numbers.
  • A learned prior that organizes the codes.A continuous normalizing flow (CNF) shapes the space of codes, so that similar aortas sit near each other and the space stays well structured.
  • A decoder that rebuilds the aorta.A second, conditional CNF turns any code back into a full 3D aorta.

Because the decoder can rebuild each aorta from its code, the code is a faithful summary of the whole shape. Reconstructions are very close to the real anatomy, with a normalized Chamfer distance below 0.001.

What we found

A small vocabulary of aortic shape

We ran principal component analysis (PCA) on the codes of all patients. Eight directions, or modes, explain 97% of the shape variation.

Each mode is easy to read. Moving along one mode changes the aorta in one recognizable way: its overall width, the length of the ascending aorta, the curve of the arch, dilation of the ascending aorta, or lengthening of the descending aorta.

We compared this with a standard autoencoder and a variational autoencoder. The CNF prior gave the most structured latent space, without losing reconstruction quality.

Marfan and Loeys-Dietz aortas have different shapes

One mode, PC5, separates the two syndromes (pFDR < 0.001).

LDS patients score higher on PC5. In plain terms, their ascending and descending aorta are more curved.

Catching unusual growth over time

Many patients had several scans over the years. We placed every scan in the learned shape space, so each patient's disease becomes a path through that space.

Next, we gave each path an anomaly score, based on how far it strays from typical growth (a Mahalanobis distance). Then we asked which shape modes drive the unusual paths.

Two modes stood out.

  • PC5, curvature. Patients with high anomaly scores showed steadily increasing curvature of the ascending aorta and bending of the descending aorta (pFDR = 0.0009, effect size r = 0.46).
  • PC6, dilation with elongation. Patients with high anomaly scores showed steady widening of the ascending aorta, together with lengthening of the upper descending aorta (pFDR = 0.029, r = 0.30).

Both are changes across several segments at once. This is the kind of coordinated remodeling that one diameter reading can't capture.

Why this matters

Diameter will stay important. But it is one number taken from a 3D organ.

Beyond Diameter turns a whole aortic shape into a short, readable code. That opens up three things.

  • Richer surveillance.Shape features that flag coordinated, multi-segment remodeling could serve as quantitative biomarkers alongside diameter.
  • A common coordinate system.Any two aortas, or any two scans of the same patient, can be compared directly in the same space.
  • Realistic geometry on demand.Because the model is generative, it can produce realistic aortic shapes and smooth in-between shapes. These can feed patient-specific simulation and digital-twin pipelines.

These results come from a single-center cohort and are exploratory. Validation on larger, multi-center data is the next step.

Technical details
  • Data. 518 aortic point clouds from CT scans of patients with MFS and LDS, Washington University in St. Louis.
  • Architecture. PointNet encoder, CNF latent prior, and conditional CNF point decoder. 16-dimensional latent space, trained for 600 epochs, ODEs solved with dopri5.
  • Baselines. Standard autoencoder (AE) and variational autoencoder (VAE).
  • Latent analysis. PCA on the aggregate posterior. Modes with eigenvalue above 0.1 were kept: eight modes, 97% of variance.
  • Group statistics. Mann-Whitney U tests with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction; rank-biserial correlation as effect size.
  • Longitudinal analysis. Per-patient latent trajectories, Mahalanobis anomaly scoring, per-mode contribution to anomaly, and per-mode rate of change (β).
